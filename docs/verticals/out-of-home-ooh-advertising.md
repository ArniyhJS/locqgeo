# Out-of-Home (OOH) Advertising

### Problem

Brands spend billions on billboards but cannot prove who saw them. Impression counts come from traffic estimates, not verified humans.

**Additional challenges:**

- Media buyers rarely get deterministic proof that a campaign drove foot traffic.
- Traditional OOH measurement relies on traffic counts that do not distinguish passengers from pedestrians.
- Competition from digital ads makes it hard to justify billboard spend without conversion metrics.
- Duplicate exposures by the same person inflate perceived reach.
- Small brands cannot afford research agencies to measure billboard effectiveness.

### How LocqGeo helps

- Billboards become interactive geofences.
- A passer-by scans a QR code; the engine confirms they are within the geofence radius.
- Brand gets verified impressions, engagement time, repeat exposure, and conversion data.

### Key features used

- Geofencing, QR scanning, device fingerprint, velocity checks, duplicate prevention, campaign budgets.

### Revenue model

- Per-verification fee.
- Campaign management SaaS for agencies.
- Share of reward spend distributed through the platform.

### Example

A telco runs 50 billboards across Lagos. Users who scan and verify get ₦100 airtime. The telco only pays for verified humans, and LocqGeo takes a per-scan fee.

**Additional examples:**

- A beverage brand places QR-enabled billboards near universities and rewards verified students with free drink coupons.
- A political campaign tracks verified interactions across rally locations and follow-up billboard exposures.
- A fintech uses OOH verification to A/B test which neighborhoods respond to its loan-product messaging.
- A movie studio rewards fans who verify at multiple poster sites with exclusive trailer access.
